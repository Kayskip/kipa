# Bonita Bigham

Sourced from her MFA exegesis, *Haehae and the art of reconciliation*, Massey University, Wellington, 2018.

## Record

Born in 1971. Daughter of Hinewaito. Great-grandchild of Neha Kipa and Ngārere. She measures that birth against Neha's return from imprisonment: just short of 100 years after his return to Taranaki in 1872.

Raised at Waiokura and, as she puts it, artistically inspired at Kātere. The exegesis is her account of cutting, haehae, as a practice running through Parihaka descendants and through her own work. It is the fullest public account these notes have of Neha, Ngārere, Apihaka, and Kātere. It is not a biography of Neha. It is an art exegesis that carries the whakapapa because the work comes from it.

She lived in Rangi Kipa's New Plymouth home in the late 1990s and learned by watching him, and Julie Paama-Pengelly. She learned tāniko through Te Wānanga o Raukawa, not from her mother, and whatu from the Taranaki weavers Puhi Nuku and Ria Waikerepuru. She is kaitiaki of Ngārere's kawe and of Apihaka's tāniko.

In 2015 she was at the Toi Oho XX wānanga marking 20 years of Māori visual arts at Massey. She later co-organised a Taranaki artists' wānanga and a short exhibition in her studio and gallery in Manaia, with Gabreille Belz. The exegesis spells the name that way.

She mentions a son, Te Rei, when writing about moko and children who do not "look Māori". No file has been made for him. The exegesis does not give a full name.
