# Rangi Kipa

Also Peter-Rangi. Earlier surname Skipper. Author of the 2010 reunion blog.

## Record

From his comment of 26 March 2010:

- One of the Waitara Skippers. He describes that branch as long settled on the Waitara and Wai-o-Ngona rivers and the Kunene reef.
- Born in 1966 in Christchurch, while his father Bert was training as a carpenter at Te Kaihanga.
- Known in the family as Peter-Rangi. He took the middle name when he went to carving school in Porirua in 1983.
- He took the surname Kipa, which he calls the original family name, when he and Julie married at the mouth of the Wai-o-Ngona on New Year's Eve 1998.
- Matāmua. Sister Kirsty followed not long after. Brothers Glen and Lionel are about five years and about ten years younger than him.
- Two children with Julie: Te Rangi Kaiwhetu (Kaiwhetu) and Te Hekerangi a Tamarau (Tamarau). In March 2010 he said Kaiwhetu was 13 as of 27 December just past, and Tamarau was 9 as of 26 February that year.
- The children spent their early years at Smart Road with Nanny kuikui Phyllis, until Phyllis died.
- In 2010 the family was living at Ohope Beach, near Whakatāne. He mentions kura kaupapa, surf lifesaving, and fishing there.
- The week before the 24 March 2010 post he was back in Taranaki after tattooing musicians at Womad, and then moko'd relatives: three of the Robinson whānau, named as Bev Gibson, Raewyn Ashby, and Lee-ana Robinson. Talk turned to Uncle Albert Skipper's tangi in February at Katere ki te Moana / Tarereare, which he had not been able to attend. That discussion is why he started the blog.

From Bonita Bigham (2018):

- She describes him as a tā moko practitioner and tohunga whakairo, the highest-profile artist in the wider whānau, working across scale from large sculpture to carved niho parāoa.
- She lived in his New Plymouth home in the late 1990s. His wife then was Julie Paama-Pengelly.
- In 2011 he completed moko kauae on his sister Ngāmata at the papakāinga at Kātere ki te Moana. Four other women received moko kauae the same day. The photograph is credited to Ngāmata Skipper.
- She quotes him, from World Indigenous Day in Ngāmotu in August 2018, on moko as a way of putting a cultural narrative back into the land.

The Te Ātiawa hapū page, as it stood in November 2026, listed Rangi Kipa among the trustees of Ngāti Tawhirikura.

Bev Gibson says his great-grandfather was John, brother of Mack.
